# Glacier "any of" rows — sources (added 2026-09-29)

Two rows built for a paired day (curated 2026-10-03, rounds 3 and 4): **Any glacier that's vanished since 1900** (57 members)
and **Any growing glacier** (3 members). Each member was researched and then independently fact-checked.

## Vanished: how members were chosen

- **NCGCP list** (Mauri Pelto, North Cascade Glacier Climate Project): all 32 entries of
  https://glaciers.nichols.edu/washington-glaciers-that-have-disappeared-since-1984-list/ . Positions decode from each GLIMS id
  (G{lon+360}E{lat}N, 0.001°). The two Snow Creek entries (0.5 km apart) are merged into one member. Peak-named entries are
  shown as "… glacier"; Lewis and Lyall are qualified "(North Cascades)" to avoid living namesakes.
- **Historic outlines** (GLIMS 1958–1985 inventories, simplified): Hinman, Anderson, Stevens, Lewis, Conrad, Glisan, Clark; Lathrop is a point.
- **Region-wide sweep** (OR, WA, BC, Alberta, Yukon/SE Alaska): only high-confidence, non-borderline members kept, plus a few
  medium ones where Pelto's post names the loss explicitly (Tenderfoot, Macbeth).
- **Mount St. Helens:** Loowit, Leschi and Wishbone, which the 18 May 1980 eruption destroyed (USGS Circular 850-D: 100% of
  Loowit and Leschi, 95% of Wishbone's area and 99% of its volume; GNIS lists all three as "(historical)"). Pinned at the GNIS
  Historical Features points, entered from the 1979 pre-eruption map. Players will think of the eruption, so these count even
  though they weren't lost to melt. Loowit's point lies inside today's Crater Glacier (a *growing* member), so a crater tap scores
  in both rounds of the paired day; both blurbs mention it.
- **Left out on purpose:** the beheaded St. Helens remnants (Forsyth, Nelson, Ape, Shoestring) and the snowfield reclassifications
  (Talus, Dryer), which rest on one remote-sensing inventory (Fountain et al. 2023) and sit within 2.5 km of the three above; Palmer (still skied as the Palmer Snowfield); borderline cases where experts disagree
  (Irving, Pyramid, Van Trump, Gotchen, Whistler, Ghost, lower Fraser); single-source or loosely placed ones (Meade, the Pasayten
  and Indian Head clusters, the glacier north of Bonnet, Dirt, Andrews, Pin Peak, Eugene/Linn/Waldo).

## Growing: members

Net growth (thickening and/or advance) within about 2015–2025. Surge-only advances (Lowell, Turner, Fisher) were
left out. Outlines are simplified OSM relations (Crater 6695149 with the lava-dome hole dropped; Hubbard 2796998; Johns Hopkins 15793192).

### Crater Glacier
Formed in the crater after the 1980 eruption. Sobolewski et al. (2023, Bulletin of Volcanology) found that snow accumulation grew it by about 13.8 million m3 from 2009 to 2019 while the toe advanced several hundred metres, and call it 'one of the world's last expanding glaciers'. An EGU Cryosphere blog post (March 2022) says it is still gaining volume and advancing north, though at a decreasing rate. Growth continued into 2024: USGS says the Cascades Volcano Observatory dismantled seismic station VALT on 6 June 2024 because it feared damage from the advancing glacier. Clear member.

### Hubbard Glacier
Hubbard has thickened and advanced since the International Boundary Commission first mapped it in 1895; USGS EROS puts the advance at about 2.4 km since 1895. Ritchie et al. (2008), cited in Motyka et al. (2008, J. Glaciol.), measured a 620 m advance from 1992 to 2006, about 35 m/yr. It dammed Russell Fiord in 1986 and 2002. Official sources inside the window still call it growing: the NPS Wrangell-St. Elias glacier article (updated 21 Dec 2023) says it 'is actually growing', and the USGS glacier research page (dated 26 Mar 2026) says it has been thickening and advancing since 1895. The one counter-signal is a large calving embayment in 2019, which Mark Fahnestock (UAF, AGU Dec 2019) called 'the first sign of weakness' in almost 50 years; it is not a documented retreat. The 'retracted since 2016' line that search engines attach to Hubbard comes from Eidam et al. (2020) on LeConte Glacier. Caveat: I found no published Hubbard terminus series after about 2016, so its 2015-2025 status rests on agency statements. As a sanity check only, not a source, Sentinel-2 cloudless composites for 2016 and 2018-2024 show the front still reaching Gilbert Point with no large retreat. Member.

- USGS (page dated 26 Mar 2026): largest tidewater glacier in North America, thickening and advancing since first mapped in 1895; dammed Russell Fiord in 1986 and 2002 — https://www.usgs.gov/programs/climate-research-and-development-program/science/additional-research-glaciers
- NPS (updated 21 Dec 2023): 'it is actually growing' — https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/wrst-glaciers.htm

### Johns Hopkins Glacier
Johns Hopkins has advanced from a minimum in the 1920s. Kaluzienski et al. (2023, Annals of Glaciology) report that since 1948 it has advanced more than 1.6 km and thickened by more than 100 m in its lower reaches, held up by a growing submarine end moraine. From 2007 to 2021 the terminus advanced about 230 m and the lower glacier thickened about 30 m, with about 190 m of that advance in 2007-2013. From 2019 to 2021 the terminus 're-stabilized' but was still slowly advancing, while the lower glacier thickened another 20 m. The NPS (page updated 1 Oct 2022) calls it 'currently the only advancing tidewater glacier on the eastern side of the Fairweather Range', and NASA Earth Observatory (Sep 2020, 2019 imagery) calls it the only tidewater glacier in the park that has been advancing in recent years. This is growth inside the window (thickening plus slow advance). Member.

- Since 1948: advanced more than 1.6 km, thickened more than 100 m in its lower reaches. 2007-2021: terminus advanced about 230 m, lower glacier thickened about 30 m. 2019-2021: terminus re-stabilized and slowly advancing, thickened 20 m (Kaluzienski et al. 2023, doi 10.1017/aog.2023.46; the 2025 erratum only corrects an author's initial) — https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/annals-of-glaciology/article/impacts-of-tidewater-glacier-advance-on-iceberg-habitat/EB9B6EA40E9A43C84F0D3636BB7C7B0C
