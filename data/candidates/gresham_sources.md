# Gresham — sources

Row added 2026-11-01 (town, medium); curated into 2026-11-02 as round 2.

**Geometry.** City limits from OpenStreetMap relation [r186274](https://www.openstreetmap.org/relation/186274)
(`boundary=administrative`, `admin_level=8`), data as of 2026-11-01, via the Overpass API. © OpenStreetMap
contributors, ODbL. One outer polygon (564 vertices; its 4 inner rings, unincorporated pockets, are ignored),
simplified with topology-preserving Douglas–Peucker at 0.15 km in a local km projection to 67 points
(max deviation 0.147 km), rounded to 4 decimals, first == last, valid simple polygon. Ring area 61.2 km²,
against Wikipedia's 61.26 km² (23.65 sq mi) total.

**Facts.**
- `population: 114,247 (2020)`: 2020 US census, from Wikipedia's infobox; the OSM relation's `population` tag
  agrees.
- `elevation_m: 117`: Wikipedia infobox (384 ft).
- `founded: 1905`: year of incorporation (Wikipedia: settled in the early 1850s by the Powell brothers,
  unincorporated until 1905). Bend and Bellingham also use incorporation years.
- `region: Multnomah County, OR`: Wikipedia infobox.

**Blurb.**
- Fourth-largest city in Oregon: 2020 census, after Portland (652,503), Eugene (176,654) and Salem (175,535);
  Hillsboro (106,447) is fifth.
- Named for Walter Q. Gresham, a Union general in the Civil War and later US Secretary of State
  (Wikipedia, *Gresham, Oregon* and *Walter Q. Gresham*).
- East Buttes: Wikipedia, *East Buttes*: "extinct volcanoes in and around Gresham, Oregon … part of the
  Boring Lava Field".
